Abstract

In the modern digital era, video conferencing has become an essential tool for communication, yet it remains dominated by foreign-owned platforms. This project, "Online Video Meeting – A Step Towards Atma Nirbhar Bharat," aims to develop a secure, robust, and indigenous video conferencing solution that aligns with the national vision of self-reliance.

The proposed platform is built using cutting-edge WebRTC technology for peer-to-peer communication, ensuring low latency and data sovereignty. A unique feature of this system is the integration of advanced Artificial Intelligence for real-time security. The platform includes a Scam Detection Engine that monitors for deepfakes, voice clones, and suspicious behavioral patterns using Random Forest classifiers. By processing these security checks entirely within the browser, the system ensures that sensitive biometric data never leaves the user's device.

Additionally, the project implements AI-driven bitrate optimization to maintain high-quality video even in low-bandwidth conditions typical of rural Indian networks. The successful implementation of this platform provides a high-security alternative to existing tools, promoting domestic technology and enhancing national digital security.

1. Introduction

The global shift toward remote work and digital interaction has made video conferencing a backbone of professional and personal life. However, most popular conferencing tools are developed and hosted outside India, raising significant concerns regarding data privacy, security, and national sovereignty. The "Atma Nirbhar Bharat" initiative encourages Indian developers to create high-quality domestic alternatives to foreign software.

This project introduces a secure, feature-rich video conferencing platform developed specifically to address the security challenges inherent in online meetings. Unlike traditional platforms that focus solely on connectivity, our solution prioritizes user safety through integrated AI. By leveraging the power of WebRTC (Real-Time Communication), we create direct connections between users, minimizing the need for central servers and thereby reducing data exposure risks.

The introduction of AI-based scam detection is a critical step forward. As scams involving deepfake technology and voice cloning become more prevalent, a platform that can identify these threats in real-time is essential. This report details the development of such a system, focusing on its architecture, objective, and the sophisticated AI algorithms that make it a superior choice for secure Indian communication.

2. Objective

The primary objective of this project is to create a self-reliant video conferencing platform that meets international standards of performance while providing superior security features. The specific objectives are:

  • To design and implement a peer-to-peer video conferencing system using WebRTC and Next.js.
  • To integrate real-time AI Scam Detection that identifies potential fraudulent activities during a live call.
  • To develop a Deepfake Detection module that uses computer vision to identify manipulated video feeds.
  • To implement Voice Clone Detection by analyzing audio frequencies for synthetic patterns.
  • To ensure Data Sovereignty by minimizing server-side processing and keeping biometric analysis on the local client.
  • To optimize video streaming quality for varied network conditions using Random Forest Bitrate Optimization.
  • To promote the Atma Nirbhar Bharat vision by providing a functional, indigenous alternative to foreign conferencing apps.

3. Problem Statement

Current video conferencing platforms face three major challenges that this project seeks to solve:

3.1 Lack of Real-Time Security Against AI Threats

Most existing apps provide encryption but do not protect against "End-Point" threats like deepfakes and voice clones. Users often have no way of knowing if the person they are talking to is physically real or an AI-generated impersonation.

3.2 Dependency and Data Sovereignty

Relying on foreign platforms means that the communication metadata and, in some cases, content are processed outside Indian jurisdiction. This creates a dependency that could be problematic during sensitive corporate or governmental discussions.

3.3 Bandwidth Inefficiency

Many conferencing tools struggle to maintain stability on low-bitrate networks common in many parts of India. Static compression algorithms often fail to adapt quickly enough to fluctuating packet loss and jitter.

4. Literature Review

4.1 Detailed Analysis of Referenced Works

[1] Afchar et al. (2018): Proposed MesoNet, a compact Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) specifically tailored to detect forged facial videos. The researchers recognized that while complex models like InceptionNet achieve high accuracy, they are computationally heavy. MesoNet uses only four convolutional layers, optimizing it for real-time analysis. Their study demonstrated a remarkably high detection accuracy of 98% on the FaceForensics dataset. In the context of our project, MesoNet's lightweight architecture makes it highly suitable for browser-based inference. We implement a MesoNet-4 ONNX model that runs efficiently on the client's machine, providing robust, real-time protection against face-swap deepfakes without server dependency.

[2] Wang et al. (2023): Introduced the concept of Diffusion Reconstruction Error (DIRE) for detecting images generated by diffusion models like DALL-E 2 and Stable Diffusion. Their research highlighted that AI-generated images have distinct frequency-domain anomalies compared to real photographs. By measuring the error when an image is compressed and reconstructed through a pre-trained diffusion model, DIRE accurately identifies synthetic media across different generators. We incorporated the core insight of DIRE—analyzing the Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) power spectrum—into our Python Flask microservice. This allows our platform to detect mid-to-high frequency artifacts typical of modern generative AI without requiring massive GPU resources locally.

[3] Ojha et al. (2023): Presented a framework for Universal Fake Image Detection leveraging the feature space of pre-trained vision-language models like CLIP. Their study revealed that AI-generated images, regardless of the generating algorithm, share underlying statistical and structural similarities. By applying a simple linear probe over CLIP features along with color and spectral statistics, they achieved state-of-the-art generalization. We adapted their findings on color channel synchronization and statistical fingerprinting for our platform. Our microservice specifically monitors color channel standard deviations and cross-channel correlations to flag suspicious video frames characteristic of GAN-induced mode collapse.

[4] Yi et al. (2022): Documented the findings of the ADD 2022 Challenge, the first major competition focused on detecting deep synthesized audio. The researchers benchmarked several models using metrics like Linear Frequency Cepstral Coefficients (LFCC) combined with ECAPA-TDNN embeddings. They found that advanced text-to-speech (TTS) and voice conversion models leave subtle acoustic clues, particularly in high-frequency bands and silence intervals. Because running their full neural network stack client-side is impractical, our platform utilizes their insights into spectral flatness and noise floor abnormalities. We employ the Web Audio API to continuously measure these factors, providing an effective heuristic defense against voice clones.

[5] Guera & Delp (2018): Were among the first to address deepfake detection as a temporal problem rather than just a spatial one. They combined a Convolutional Neural Network to extract frame-level features with a Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network to evaluate sequence coherence. Their approach yielded over 97% accuracy, proving that manipulated videos inevitably exhibit frame-to-frame inconsistencies and unnatural temporal transitions. This temporal perspective directly inspired our Optical Flow detection parameter. Instead of a heavy LSTM, our platform uses a high-speed Lucas-Kanade block-matching algorithm at 5 frames per second to analyze face rigidity and motion coherence, successfully identifying "puppet-master" style real-time deepfakes.

[6] Rossler et al. (2019): Created FaceForensics++, an extensive dataset of over 1,000 manipulated videos specifically designed to train and benchmark deepfake detection models. They evaluated various detection strategies across different compression levels. Their most critical finding was that heavy video compression, typical in real-time video conferencing, severely degrades spatial detection accuracy, making temporal analysis much more reliable. We integrated this understanding into our design by utilizing a rolling 30-frame motion history window. This guarantees that our algorithm evaluates motion consistency over time rather than relying solely on individual, potentially compressed or degraded frames.

[7] Mao et al. (2017): Developed Pensieve, a system that employs reinforcement learning (RL) to produce adaptive bitrate (ABR) algorithms for video streaming. Pensieve trains a neural network to observe network state features—such as Round Trip Time (RTT), buffer size, and packet loss—and dynamically select the optimal bitrate. Their system outperformed traditional rule-based methods by significantly improving the Quality of Experience (QoE). Drawing inspiration from Pensieve's feature-selection, our platform implements a pre-trained Random Forest ensemble model. It continuously evaluates real-time WebRTC metrics (RTT, jitter, drops) to adaptively scale video resolution and bitrate, ensuring maximum stability for volatile networks.

[8] Muller et al. (2022): Investigated the generalization capabilities of audio deepfake detection models. They analyzed how synthesized speech systems struggle to accurately replicate human prosody and micro-intonations. Specifically, they noted that the Zero-Crossing Rate (ZCR) variance in AI-cloned voices is unnaturally stable compared to genuine human speech, which naturally fluctuates due to vocal cord dynamics. We directly translated this acoustic insight into our live voice clone detection engine. By computing the rolling standard deviation of the ZCR on the user’s live microphone input, our platform can immediately flag maliciously injected synthetic audio based on abnormal pitch regularity.

[9] Corvi et al. (2023): Probed the forensic signatures left by state-of-the-art diffusion models. They demonstrated that while diffusion models generate visually stunning images, they produce distinct, unnatural spatial frequency patterns—visible as periodic peaks in the Fourier or DCT domains. These synthetic fingerprints occur because of the iterative denoising processes used during image generation. We leverage this exact finding within our platform’s optional Python-Flask microservice. By computing the 2D DCT of incoming video frames and evaluating the ratio of low-to-high frequency energy, our system can heuristically spot these periodic artifacts, identifying diffusion-based virtual camera injections.

[10] WebRTC Working Group (2021): Published the WebRTC 1.0 specification, standardizing real-time communication capabilities directly within web browsers through native APIs. By defining precise protocols for ICE peer discovery, STUN/TURN traversal, and DTLS-SRTP encryption, WebRTC enables high-performance, plugin-free peer-to-peer data and media exchange. Our platform leverages this standard to achieve end-to-end media encryption and near-zero latency, circumventing the need for expensive, privacy-intrusive central media servers. By accessing raw incoming and outgoing MediaStreamTrack data via WebRTC APIs, we unlock the ability to run our AI security pipelines directly on the client, upholding the core data sovereignty tenets.

[11] Government of India (2020): Launched the Atma Nirbhar Bharat Abhiyan (Self-Reliant India Initiative) to promote national self-sufficiency across economic and technological sectors. Specifically, the initiative calls for the development of indigenous digital infrastructure to reduce reliance on foreign technology monopolies, secure national data sovereignty, and protect citizen privacy. This policy serves as the core motivation for our project. By building a video conferencing platform completely from open-source technologies, hosting it locally, and ensuring that no sensitive biometric or communication data ever leaves the Indian network boundary, our project operationalizes the technical vision of a secure, digital, and self-reliant India.

5. Conclusion

The development of the "Online Video Meeting – Atma Nirbhar Bharat" platform marks a significant milestone in indigenous software development. By integrating sophisticated AI security checks into a standard WebRTC framework, we have created a solution that is not only functional but also inherently safer than many market leaders.

The platform successfully detects deepfakes, voice clones, and network anomalies in real-time. This provides users with a critical layer of defense against modern cyber threats, all while ensuring that their data remains localized and private. This project proves that Indian technological innovation can compete at a global scale, providing professional-grade tools for a self-reliant nation.
